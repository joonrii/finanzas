# Competitive Research

> Research updated: 2026-08-19. Sources: Apple App Store listings/reviews and official product pages where available.

## Executive conclusion

Our strongest positioning opportunity is **not** to become another full personal-finance suite. It is to combine:

- the **speed and simplicity of Monefy**;
- the **visual quality, animations and merchant presentation of Margen**;
- the useful foundations expected from modern trackers (accounts, recurring transactions, CSV, analytics);
- a deliberately narrow scope.

Core hypothesis:

> **People who want to manually track their finances may prefer a beautiful, modern app that makes the habit pleasant, without the complexity of a banking/financial super-app.**

This is a product hypothesis, not yet validated market evidence.

# 1. Monefy

## Current positioning

Monefy explicitly focuses on making expense tracking easy: the App Store description says users can add expenses such as a coffee or taxi and emphasizes very fast entry. It supports multiple devices/partner use and is available in Spanish plus 15 other languages. The Spanish App Store currently shows 4.6/5 from about 3.7k ratings. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/es/app/monefy-control-de-gastos/id1212024409)

## What users like

The review corpus reinforces the core value proposition: users repeatedly describe the app as easy, functional and useful for visualizing daily spending and identifying unnecessary spending.

## Problems / opportunities

Recent Spanish reviews include complaints around aggressive premium prompts, pricing/promotion confusion, and multi-device/subscription behaviour. One review specifically says the free version felt too limited and another complains about repeated attempts to push Premium. These are individual reviews, not proof of a general market problem.

## What we should learn

**Copy:**
- minimal mental model;
- fast amount-first entry;
- low cognitive load;
- focus on the daily habit.

**Improve:**
- visual polish;
- animations;
- merchant logos;
- modern dashboard;
- clearer free/premium boundary;
- robust account/data portability.

### Strategic conclusion

**Monefy is our closest product competitor.** We should be able to explain why someone would choose us in one sentence:

> "It's as easy as Monefy, but it looks and feels like a premium modern fintech app."

# 2. Margen

## Current positioning

Margen's App Store message is essentially **"all your money in one number"**. It combines bank accounts, investments, crypto, property and debts into net worth, and also supports movements, budgets, recurring transactions, investment prices, multi-currency and shared accounts. Premium is currently listed at €4.99/month or €39.99/year. The Spanish App Store shows 4.3/5 from 11 ratings, so its local review volume is still very small.

## What we should learn

Margen is the strongest reference for:

- visual hierarchy;
- animations;
- modern fintech aesthetics;
- net-worth storytelling;
- merchant/logotype presentation;
- making financial data feel enjoyable rather than administrative.

## What we should NOT copy

We do not need:

- property;
- crypto;
- mortgages/simulators;
- complex investment tracking;
- broad asset management;
- bank aggregation.

### Strategic conclusion

**Margen defines our visual quality bar, not our feature roadmap.**

Our product should feel like a simpler cousin rather than a smaller copy.

# 3. Soldi

## Current positioning

Soldi positions itself as a simple personal-finance and budget planner. It supports manual income/expense entry, budgets, categories/subcategories, multiple wallets, transfers, notes, attachments, charts, recurring transactions, shared expenses, debts, multi-currency, dark mode and CSV export. It currently has 4.8/5 from about 1.1k Spanish App Store ratings and is available in Spanish, English and Italian according to the listing.

Its recent releases show a strong focus on onboarding, faster transaction entry, smoother UI, internationalization and visual polish.

## Review signals

A recent review asks for bank connection because entering transactions one by one is too slow for that user. Another negative review calls the premium price excessive. Again, these are individual reviews, but they highlight two recurring category tensions:

1. manual entry can become tedious at scale;
2. users are sensitive to the perceived value of subscriptions.

## What we should learn

Useful features to benchmark:

- recurring transactions;
- CSV import/export;
- multi-currency;
- fast transaction entry;
- good empty states;
- onboarding/tutorial;
- internationalization.

## What we should avoid

Do not automatically add:

- budgets;
- shared debt tracking;
- extensive wallet management;
- attachments;
- many advanced financial workflows.

Only add them if user research proves they support our core habit.

# 4. Wallet by BudgetBakers

## Current positioning

Wallet is the broadest/more mature competitor in this set. It combines income/expense tracking, budgets, multi-device synchronization, currencies, categories, investments, stocks/ETFs and bank synchronization. The Spanish App Store lists 4.4/5 from about 974 ratings and 47 languages.

The listing currently shows €1.99/month and €14.99/year for Premium in the Spanish App Store, although pricing and offers can vary by region and change over time.

## Review signals

Reviews include complaints about bank reconnection and limitations caused by synchronized bank transactions, such as inability to change transaction dates in certain situations. There are also requests around widgets and financial-month configuration.

These are particularly interesting for us because they show a downside of bank synchronization: automation is powerful, but it can also reduce user control.

## What we should learn

Wallet proves that users value:

- broad financial visibility;
- synchronization;
- multi-device support;
- investment tracking;
- mature reporting.

But it also reinforces our strategic decision:

> **We should not compete on feature count.**

# 5. Gestor de gastos, ingresos (Orange Dog)

The Spanish App Store listing currently shows 4.9/5 from about 4.7k ratings. It emphasizes quick transaction entry, automatic balance calculation, charts, detailed reports, custom categories and multi-currency with real-time exchange rates. It is available in Spanish and 36 more languages.

## What we should learn

This proves there is substantial demand for the straightforward manual-tracking category and for international/multi-currency support.

## Opportunity

The product proposition is more generic than the visual/brand direction we want. We can differentiate through:

- stronger brand;
- premium visual identity;
- merchant recognition;
- animations;
- better onboarding;
- clearer product story.

# 6. Registro Contable: Gastos / Money Manager (Realbyte)

The App Store listing shows 4.8/5 from about 2.3k ratings. It supports income, expenses, monthly balances, multiple accounts, multiple currencies, weekly/monthly/yearly statistics, calendar and budgets. It supports Spanish plus 18 languages. Current Spanish App Store pricing shown in the listing includes €2.49/month and €21.49/year.

## What we should learn

This is evidence that a utilitarian manual money-management product can build a meaningful review base over time.

## Opportunity

Our differentiation should be the opposite of an accounting tool:

> **financial clarity without feeling like bookkeeping.**

# Cross-competitor findings

## 1. Simplicity is valuable

Monefy's reviews strongly validate the appeal of fast, simple expense tracking. Multiple competitors also market themselves around ease of use.

**Decision:** simplicity is a core product principle, not a temporary MVP limitation.

## 2. Visual quality is a genuine opportunity

Margen demonstrates how financial information can be presented as a premium consumer product rather than a spreadsheet. Our own product insight came from using Monefy and finding it functional but visually unattractive.

**Decision:** visual design is one of our primary competitive advantages.

## 3. Manual entry remains viable

Several competitors still offer manual-first tracking. Users may eventually ask for automation, but manual entry itself is not a dead category.

**Decision:** no Open Banking in the initial roadmap.

## 4. Manual entry has a ceiling

Soldi reviews show that some users want bank connection because manual entry becomes tedious. This is important for our product strategy.

**Decision:** make manual entry exceptionally fast and investigate iOS Shortcuts/widgets as future automation, rather than adding bank aggregation.

## 5. Internationalization is a competitive requirement

Competitors support many languages. If we want an international product, localization cannot be bolted on later.

**Decision:** English first-class from the beginning, with Spanish/French and then German/Italian as high-priority languages.

## 6. Subscription perception matters

Monefy and Soldi both have reviews complaining about premium pricing or paywall pressure.

**Decision:** the free product must be genuinely useful. Premium should sell a better experience and advanced capabilities, not basic financial hygiene.

## 7. Feature bloat is not our route

Wallet, Margen and Soldi demonstrate how quickly the category expands into budgets, investments, property, debt, banking and shared finances.

**Decision:** every new feature must justify itself against the core job-to-be-done.

# Our competitive position

## The product we want

**Monefy's simplicity + Margen's visual quality + enough financial depth to understand personal money.**

### Core job-to-be-done

> "I want to know where my money is, where it goes and how my overall financial position is changing, without having to manage a complicated finance system."

### Product pillars

1. **Beautiful** — premium consumer-app quality.
2. **Simple** — minimal navigation and cognitive load.
3. **Fast** — a transaction should take seconds.
4. **Clear** — show useful information without overwhelming the user.
5. **Controlled** — manual data entry and CSV rather than mandatory bank access.
6. **International** — designed for multiple currencies, languages and markets.
